Chemistry Practical Quiz

Structured laboratory evaluation with crisp, high-contrast layouts.

1.
During a titration, why is a white tile placed directly underneath the conical flask?
The white tile provides a clean background so the colour change of the indicator at the endpoint can be observed clearly against a neutral surface.
2.
Which of the following indicators is considered most suitable for the titration of a strong acid against a weak base?
Methyl Orange is preferred because its pH transition range (3.1–4.4) aligns with the acidic equivalence point of a strong acid–weak base titration.
3.
When a few drops of dilute hydrochloric acid are added to an unknown salt, a colorless, odorless gas is evolved that turns lime water milky. Which anion is most likely present in the salt?
The gas is carbon dioxide, produced from the carbonate (CO₃²⁻) or hydrogen carbonate (HCO₃⁻) anion reacting with acid, which turns limewater milky by forming CaCO₃ precipitate.
4.
If concentrated acid is accidentally spilled on the laboratory bench, what is the safest initial action to take?
Flood the spill area with copious amounts of water to dilute the acid, then wipe up with a suitable absorbent material and neutralize if needed.
